4. Sample size ladder

4 times the people, half the margin. The square root is why: to cut the margin in half you must quadruple n.

This uses the safe rule at p = 50%, the case that needs the most people. Cost note: each person you reach costs time and money, so going from plus-or-minus 3 to plus-or-minus 1.5 costs about four times as much, not twice.

6. What the margin of error does NOT cover

  • Bias. If the sample leaves out part of the population, a bigger sample just repeats the same mistake.
  • Nonresponse. If the people who answer differ from the people who do not, the result is off by an amount the formula cannot see.
  • Bad wording. A confusing or leading question shifts answers. No margin of error can fix that.
  • Chance misses. The margin of error is built to hold about 95 times in 100. That means about 5 polls in 100 miss by more than the margin by chance alone, even when the poll is done perfectly.
