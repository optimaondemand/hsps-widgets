Lesson 21.3
Data Displays in Excel (Lab)
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Excel draws what you tell it to draw — and your job is the part it cannot do.
A spreadsheet lab: column, pie, scatter, and line charts built honestly
Reading & work: Keep this page open beside Excel. Work through Parts A to D in order, build the workbook, and upload it to Canvas. Plan on about 65 minutes.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to build a column chart, a circle graph, a scatter chart, and a line graph in Excel from tables of data.
2
I will be able to label every chart with a title and axis titles, and check where each axis starts.
3
I will be able to explain why a line graph should be built from the scatter chart type when the time gaps are true values.
4
I will be able to audit your own charts with the five-question checklist and record what Excel's default got wrong.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
A notebook and pencil.
▶
Microsoft Excel (desktop or web) and your OneDrive Optima → Math folder.
▶
The free Desmos Scientific Calculator, open in another tab: desmos.com/scientific.
💻 THE LAB
Today's lesson is a lab. You will build four charts in Excel from the datasets you have already met — a column chart, a circle graph, a scatter chart, and a line graph — and then check each one against the five questions from Lesson 21.2. You finish with one workbook, which you upload to the Canvas assignment. Excel work happens outside Canvas: keep this page open beside Excel and follow the steps.
A theme runs through the whole lab: Excel will happily draw a chart that breaks the rules. Its defaults are conveniences, not judgments. Your job is the part the software cannot do — making sure the picture is honest.
📝 BEFORE YOU START
▶ Open Excel and start a Blank workbook. Save it right away as Wk21_Lab_YourName in your OneDrive Optima → Math folder.
▶ You will use four sheets. Create them with the + button at the bottom of the window and rename each by double-clicking its tab: Sports, Study, Tea, Notes.
▶ Menu names below are for desktop Excel. In Excel for the web the same buttons appear on the Insert tab; if one is in a slightly different place, look for the same icon name.
📊 PART A — THE COLUMN CHART AND THE CIRCLE GRAPH (SHEET: SPORTS)
Enter the data. In A1 type Sport and in B1 type Students. Starting in row 2, type the five sports and counts: Soccer, 14; Basketball, 11; Swimming, 8; Tennis, 5; Volleyball, 2. In A7 type Total and in B7 type =SUM(B2:B6): it must show 40.
Column chart.
▶ Click and drag to select A1:B6 (not the total row).
▶ Click the Insert tab. In the Charts group click Insert Column or Bar Chart, then choose Clustered Column. A chart appears.
▶ Click the chart title and type a real title, such as Favorite Sport of 40 Surveyed Students (constructed data).
▶ Click the chart, then click the green + (Chart Elements) at its upper right and tick Axis Titles. Click each axis title and replace it: Sport for the horizontal axis and Number of students for the vertical axis.
▶ Check the axis start. Right-click the numbers on the vertical axis, choose Format Axis, and look under Axis Options at Bounds → Minimum. It must say 0. Excel normally starts column charts at zero, but when all the values are close together it can pick a higher starting value by itself, so always look. If it is not 0, type 0 and press Enter.
Circle graph.
▶ Select A1:B6 again. On the Insert tab click Insert Pie or Doughnut Chart and choose Pie.
▶ Add a title as before. Click the + and tick Data Labels; right-click a label, choose Format Data Labels, and under Label Options tick Percentage (you may untick Value). You should see 35%, 27.5% (it may show 28%), 20%, 12.5% (13%), and 5%.
▶ Move each chart so it sits beside its data without covering any cells.
Check. Which bar is tallest? Does the pie's biggest slice match? Do the percents total 100%? (If Excel rounds 27.5% and 12.5% up, they will total 101% — the rounding point from Lesson 19.2.)
🔵 PART B — THE SCATTER CHART (SHEET: STUDY)
Enter the data. In A1 type Hours studied and in B1 Quiz score. In rows 2–11 enter the ten pairs: 1, 52; 1, 60; 2, 58; 2.5, 66; 3, 70; 3.5, 68; 4, 76; 4.5, 80; 5, 79; 6, 88. The explanatory variable goes in the first column — Excel puts the first column on the horizontal axis.
▶ Select A1:B11. On the Insert tab, click Insert Scatter (X, Y) or Bubble Chart and choose Scatter (the first icon: dots only, no lines).
▶ Add a title (Hours Studied and Quiz Score (constructed data)) and both axis titles with + → Axis Titles: Hours studied and Quiz score (out of 100).
▶ Right-click the vertical axis, choose Format Axis, and set Minimum to 40 and Maximum to 100 if you wish. A scatter plot plots positions, not lengths, so starting above zero is acceptable when the axis is labeled. Leave the horizontal axis starting at 0.
▶ Delete the legend if one appears (click it and press Delete): there is only one series.
Check. Compare with the scatter plot in Lesson 21.1. Write in the Notes sheet the direction, form, strength, and any outlier — and one sentence saying that the plot shows association, not cause.
📈 PART C — THE LINE GRAPH (SHEET: TEA)
Enter the data. In A1 type Minutes and in B1 Temperature (C). In rows 2–7 enter: 0, 90; 2, 78; 4, 70; 6, 64; 8, 60; 10, 57.
A trap to avoid. Excel has a plain Line chart, but it spaces its points evenly and treats the first column as labels (or, worse, as a second line). That is exactly Distortion 3. To space the points by their true values, build the line graph from the scatter chart type:
▶ Select A1:B7. On the Insert tab, click Insert Scatter (X, Y) or Bubble Chart and choose Scatter with Straight Lines and Markers.
▶ Add the title (Tea Temperature After Pouring (constructed data)) and axis titles (Minutes since poured, Temperature (°C)).
▶ Check both axes in Format Axis. The horizontal axis should run 0 to 10. A vertical axis starting above zero is acceptable on a line graph if it is labeled; note in Notes what your minimum is.
Check. Does the line fall steeply and then flatten, as in Lesson 21.1? In the Notes sheet, record the drop in the first two minutes and in the last two (12 and 3).
✅ PART D — THE HONESTY CHECK (SHEET: NOTES)
In the Notes sheet, make a small table with these columns: Chart, Zero start?, Full range?, Equal gaps?, Sizes true?, Labels, source?. Fill one row for each of your four charts with Yes, No, or Not applicable. Then add, below the table, two sentences: the one thing Excel's default got wrong (or nearly wrong) on one of your charts, and what you did about it. If nothing went wrong, say what you checked and why it matters.
Save the workbook. Upload the .xlsx file to the Canvas assignment. Do not paste screenshots of the charts into the assignment instead; the teacher needs the live workbook to see the formulas and settings.
💻 CALCULATOR SKILLS · YOUR EXCEL TOOLKIT SO FAR
Typed cleanly: variable names in row 1, one row per individual, the explanatory variable first. Checks: =SUM() for totals, =COUNT() for how many numbers. Counting: =COUNTIF() and =COUNTIFS() from last week. Charts: Insert tab → Charts group. Honesty settings: chart title, axis titles, and Format Axis → Bounds to check where a vertical axis starts. Desmos plays a small part this week: for example (57-90)/10 → −3.3 for the tea's average cooling rate in °C per minute.
🤖 AI TRAINING · PROMPT → PREDICT → VERIFY
Prompt. Ask an AI tool for the Excel steps to build a scatter chart from two columns of data. Follow its instructions literally, one step at a time, and write down every step that does not match what you see on your screen.
Predict. Predict where it will go wrong: menu names change between versions, and a tool that cannot see your screen cannot know which Excel you have. Will it remember to tell you which column goes on the horizontal axis?
Verify. Count the mismatches and rate each as harmless (different menu name) or harmful (a step that would produce a wrong chart). Note whether it mentioned checking the axis start, labels, and units — the part that matters most for honesty.
⚡ TODAY'S ACTIVITIES (about 45 minutes total)
1
Build the Four Charts
25 min
1
Follow Parts A, B, and C to build the column chart, the circle graph, the scatter chart, and the line graph. Check every total and every axis as you go.
2
Compare each chart with the version drawn in the lessons. If anything differs (a different tick spacing, different colors), decide whether the difference changes the meaning.
▶  Check yourself
Sports: column heights 14, 11, 8, 5, 2 with the axis starting at 0; the pie's largest slice is soccer at 35%; the data total 40. Study: ten points rising from lower left to upper right; the axes labeled. Tea: a line falling from 90 to 57, steep at first and then flat, with the horizontal axis running 0 to 10.
2
Complete the Honesty Check
10 min
1
Fill in the Notes sheet table from Part D for all four charts, and write the two-sentence reflection.
2
Ask a partner (or imagine one) to read only your chart titles and axis labels and tell you what each chart shows. Fix anything unclear.
▶  Check
Column and pie charts: Yes to zero start (for the column chart), N/A for full range and equal gaps; the pie needs the data total to match the percents. Scatter: N/A for zero start is acceptable if labeled; line graph: full range Yes, equal gaps Yes because of the scatter type. The reflection should name something specific, such as the axis start Excel picked.
3
Submit and Reflect
10 min
1
Save the workbook as Wk21_Lab_YourName and upload it to the Canvas assignment.
2
In two sentences, say which of the five questions you would be most likely to forget, and how you will remember to ask it.
