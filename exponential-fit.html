      How the fit is made
      
The fit uses ordinary least squares on the pairs (x, ln y). It chooses the line ln y = c + d x that makes the sum of squared vertical gaps on the log scale as small as possible. The intercept c is ln a and the slope d is ln b. Undoing the log gives a = ec and b = ed, so the model is ŷ = a · bx.

      
Each step of 1 in x multiplies ŷ by b. The percent change per step is (b − 1) × 100. The doubling time is ln 2 ÷ ln b when b > 1. The halving time is ln 2 ÷ |ln b| when b < 1. The r and r² shown for the exponential model are measured on the log scale, using ln y.

      
This method minimises gaps on the log scale, not gaps on the original scale, so other fitting methods can give slightly different a and b. Real growth often slows down as limits appear (food, space, customers), so an exponential model describes a stretch of growth, not growth forever.
