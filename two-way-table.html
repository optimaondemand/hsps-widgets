Click a cell for a joint probability, a row or column total for a marginal one. In a conditional view, clicking a cell shows the conditional probability instead.

Each row as a segmented bar (column shares within the row)

What to watch: The same table answers three different questions. Joint: what share of everyone sits in this cell? Marginal: what share lands in this row (or column), ignoring the other variable entirely? Conditional: among one row only, how do the columns split? If the segmented bars look the same from row to row, the two variables are telling you nothing about each other — that is what independence looks like in data. Bars that differ row-to-row are evidence of association (though never, by themselves, of causation).
