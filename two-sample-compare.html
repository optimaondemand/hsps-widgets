Compare Two Groups

Every dataset here is made up for practice, and every resample or shuffle is done by your browser. Nothing here is real data.

1. Choose the data

2. Look at the two groups

Group A (blue circles) Group B (orange squares) Dashed line = group mean
Which tab fits?
  • Two groups came from two random samples of two populations: you want to estimate the difference between the populations. Use the first tab.
  • One set of people or things was split into two groups by random assignment to two treatments: ask whether chance alone could explain the gap. Use the second tab.

3. Bootstrap the difference

Each resample draws a new Group A and a new Group B with replacement from the values you actually have (same group sizes), then records the new difference (B minus A). Repeat many times to see how much the difference swings from sample to sample.

Press a Resample button to begin.

3. Shuffle the group labels

Pretend the treatment made no difference. Then every value would have landed in the same place no matter which group it was assigned to. Each shuffle pools all the values, deals them back out at random into groups of the original sizes, and records the new difference (B minus A). This shows the gaps that chance alone produces.

Gaps smaller than the one we saw Gaps at least as big, in either direction Gold line = observed difference, gold dashed = its mirror image
Press a Shuffle button to begin.
Keep in mind
  • These tools describe the data you give them. They do not repair bias, a poor sample, or data that was not collected at random.
  • Resampling and shuffling use chance on purpose. A new run gives slightly different numbers, and a bigger count of resamples or shuffles gives a steadier picture.
  • Judging how big or how important a difference is takes more than these pictures: look at the size of the gap and what it means for the real situation.
