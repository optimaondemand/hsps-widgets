The bookie’s offers (priced at your rates)

Bet A: pays $1.00 if it rains, $0 otherwise.
Since you say P(Rain) = 0.60, you consider a fair price to be $0.60.
Bet B: pays $1.00 if it does not rain, $0 otherwise.
Since you say P(No Rain) = 0.60, you consider a fair price to be $0.60.
By your own lights both prices are fair — so you should be willing to buy both bets. Total cost: $1.20. Exactly one of them will pay $1.00.

What to watch: Whenever your two probabilities total more than 1, the ledger shows the same red number in both rows — you lose no matter what the sky does. No bad luck involved; your own beliefs priced the bets. Slide the total to exactly 1 and the guaranteed leak stops. That is the Dutch book argument: the probability axioms aren’t arbitrary rules — they are self-defense for anyone willing to act on their beliefs.
